The Confluence Projects

We measure extreme heat and cold where people live, and study how it shapes health as we age.

The Confluence Projects bring clinical informatics, environmental data science, and biostatistics together across Mass General Brigham, Harvard Medical School, and the Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health. We build high-resolution exposure data, link it to health records, and ask how heat, cold, and air pollution affect dementia and the body's capacity to recover from illness.

What we study

Exposure where people live

Assigning each person the reading from the nearest weather station misclassifies who was exposed. GridEX estimates daily apparent temperature and extreme heat and cold events on a 500 m grid, so exposure can be linked to health data at any geographic scale. Other NIH-funded studies now build on it.

How GridEX works

Recovery and resilience in aging

Older adults differ in how fully and how quickly they recover after an acute illness. Using longitudinal health records, we measure recovery capacity across organ systems and test whether environmental exposure and inflammation wear it down over time.

Dementia and the exposome

With support from the National Institute on Aging, we identify dementia and its precursors in clinical records with AI and examine how long-term environmental exposures relate to its onset and course.

GridEX is open data

A national catalog of extreme heat and cold events for the contiguous United States, with daily temperature, dew point, wind, and apparent temperature surfaces. Raster layers come as GeoTIFF and event boundaries as GeoPackage, under the MIT license.

Described in Environmental Science & Technology (2025).
